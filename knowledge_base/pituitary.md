# Clinical Guidelines & Pathology: Pituitary Adenoma / Neuroendocrine Tumor (PitNET)

## 1. Overview & Classification
Pituitary adenomas (now officially termed PitNETs - Pituitary Neuroendocrine Tumors by WHO) arise from the anterior pituitary gland (adenohypophysis). Almost all are benign (non-cancerous), but they cause morbidity through mass effect (compression of the optic chiasm) or systemic hormone hypersecretion.

### Classification by Size:
- **Microadenoma**: Diameter < 10 mm (< 1.0 cm).
- **Macroadenoma**: Diameter ≥ 10 mm (≥ 1.0 cm) — frequently compresses the optic chiasm.
- **Giant Adenoma**: Diameter ≥ 40 mm (≥ 4.0 cm).

### Functional (Hormone-Secreting) Subtypes:
1. **Prolactinoma (30-40%)**: Secretes prolactin. Causes galactorrhea, amenorrhea, infertility in females; loss of libido and erectile dysfunction in males.
2. **Somatotroph (Growth Hormone / GH) Adenoma (15-20%)**: Causes Acromegaly in adults and Gigantism in children. Associated with cardiomegaly and diabetes.
3. **Corticotroph (ACTH) Adenoma (10-15%)**: Causes Cushing's Disease (central obesity, moon facies, hypertension, striae, osteoporosis).
4. **Thyrotroph (TSH) Adenoma (<2%)**: Rare cause of secondary hyperthyroidism.
5. **Non-Functioning Pituitary Adenoma (NFPA, 30-35%)**: Does not secrete hormones. Often presents late with headaches and bitemporal hemianopsia (loss of peripheral vision).

## 2. Treatment & Management Pathways

### Medical Therapy (First-line for Prolactinomas):
- **Dopamine Agonists**: Cabergoline (preferred due to high efficacy and tolerability) or Bromocriptine.
- Shrinks tumor mass in >85% of cases, often avoiding the need for surgical intervention.

### Surgical Intervention (First-line for Non-Prolactinomas and Compressive Macroadenomas):
- **Endoscopic Endonasal Transsphenoidal Surgery (EETS)**: Minimally invasive approach through the nasal cavity to resect the tumor from the sella turcica without opening the skull.
- Indicated for optic chiasm compression, progressive visual loss, Cushing's disease, and Acromegaly.

### Radiation & Radiosurgery:
- Stereotactic Radiosurgery (Gamma Knife / CyberKnife) for residual or recurrent adenomas in the cavernous sinus where surgical access is limited.

---

## 3. Pre- and Post-Operative Evaluation
- **Endocrine Workup**: Complete baseline hormone panel: Serum Prolactin, IGF-1, 24-hr urinary free cortisol (UFC) or low-dose dexamethasone suppression test, morning ACTH, Free T4/TSH, LH, FSH, and Testosterone/Estradiol.
- **Neuro-Ophthalmology Assessment**: Formal Goldman or Humphrey automated visual field testing to evaluate optic chiasm compromise.
- **Fluid & Electrolyte Monitoring**: High risk of transient or permanent Diabetes Insipidus (DI) and SIADH post-surgery; strict monitoring of urine output and serum sodium levels.
