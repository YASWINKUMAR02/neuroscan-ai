# Patient Care, Brain MRI & Recovery FAQs

## 1. Understanding Brain MRI Results
- **What is Gadolinium Contrast?**: A safe contrast agent given intravenously during an MRI scan to make blood vessels and active tumor margins light up brightly on T1-weighted images. It is filtered out by the kidneys.
- **What does "No Tumor" (Normal Scan) mean?**: The MRI shows symmetrical brain anatomy with no abnormal contrast enhancement, mass effect, midline shift, or vasogenic edema.
- **What is Tumor Surface Area & Volume?**: The AI measures the cross-sectional area of the lesion in square centimeters (cm²). A smaller area generally correlates with localized disease, while larger areas require comprehensive neurosurgical review.

---

## 2. Questions Patients Should Ask Their Specialist
1. What exact type of brain tumor do the imaging features suggest?
2. Is the tumor benign (non-cancerous) or malignant (aggressive)?
3. What are my treatment choices (e.g. active monitoring with periodic MRI, surgery, medication, or radiation)?
4. Is surgery necessary, and if so, what kind of surgical approach is recommended?
5. What are the potential risks, recovery timeline, and expected hospital stay?
6. Do I need hormone blood tests or visual field tests?
7. Should I obtain a second opinion at a dedicated neuro-oncology center?

---

## 3. Lifestyle, Recovery & Red Flag Symptoms
- **Warning Symptoms Requiring Immediate Emergency Care**:
  - Sudden new or worsening severe headache.
  - New onset seizure or convulsion.
  - Sudden weakness, numbness, or loss of motor control on one side of the body.
  - Sudden vision changes, double vision, or loss of peripheral vision.
  - Confusion, personality alterations, or extreme lethargy.
  - High fever or persistent clear fluid drainage from the nose (CSF leak after nasal surgery).
- **Driving Guidelines**: Following a brain tumor diagnosis, seizure, or brain surgery, medical driving restrictions apply per local transportation authority guidelines (typically 6–12 months seizure-free).
- **Emotional Support**: Patients and families are strongly encouraged to seek neuro-oncology patient support groups and psychological counseling.
