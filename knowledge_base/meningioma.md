# Clinical Guidelines & Pathology: Meningioma (WHO CNS 5 Classification)

## 1. Overview & Classification
Meningiomas arise from arachnoid cap cells of the meninges and are the most common primary non-glial intracranial tumors. Over 80% are benign (WHO Grade 1), but recurrence and aggressive behavior occur in higher grades.

### WHO CNS 5 Grading System:
1. **WHO Grade 1 (Benign / Low Grade, ~80-85%)**:
   - Subtypes: Meningothelial, Fibrous (fibroblastic), Transitional, Psammomatous, Angiomatous, Microcystic, Secretory.
   - Low mitotic rate (<4 mitoses / 10 HPF), absence of brain invasion.
2. **WHO Grade 2 (Atypical / Intermediate Grade, ~15-20%)**:
   - Mitotic count of 4–19 mitoses / 10 HPF, or brain parenchymal invasion, or ≥3 aggressive histologic criteria (spontaneous necrosis, sheeting, prominent nucleoli, high cellularity, small cells).
   - Specific histological subtypes: Chordoid, Clear cell.
3. **WHO Grade 3 (Anaplastic / Malignant Grade, ~1-3%)**:
   - Mitotic count ≥20 mitoses / 10 HPF, or frank malignancy resembling sarcoma/carcinoma.
   - Specific subtypes: Papillary, Rhabdoid.
   - Defined by TERT promoter mutation and/or homozygous deletion of CDKN2A/B.

## 2. Surgical Resection & Simpson Scale
Maximal safe resection is the cornerstone of treatment:
- **Simpson Grade I**: Complete removal of tumor, dural attachment, and abnormal bone (lowest recurrence ~9% at 10 years).
- **Simpson Grade II**: Complete removal of tumor and coagulation of dural attachment (~19% recurrence).
- **Simpson Grade III**: Complete removal without resection or coagulation of dural attachment (~29% recurrence).
- **Simpson Grade IV**: Subtotal or partial resection (~40% recurrence).
- **Simpson Grade V**: Simple decompression / biopsy.

---

## 3. Radiation Therapy & Radiosurgery
- **Stereotactic Radiosurgery (SRS / Gamma Knife / CyberKnife)**:
  - Indicated for small to medium (<3 cm) skull base meningiomas (cavernous sinus, petroclival), residual tumors, or medically inoperable patients.
  - Typical marginal dose: 12–16 Gy.
- **Fractionated External Beam Radiotherapy (EBRT)**:
  - Standard adjuvant therapy for WHO Grade 2 (selected subtotal or high-risk cases) and WHO Grade 3 (54–60 Gy in 1.8–2.0 Gy fractions).

---

## 4. Patient Guidance & FAQ
- **Is meningioma cancer?**: The vast majority (>80%) are non-cancerous (benign) and curable with surgery or controllable with radiation.
- **When is 'Watchful Waiting' (Active Surveillance) appropriate?**: Small, asymptomatic, incidentally discovered meningiomas in older adults can be monitored with serial MRI scans (at 3, 6, and 12 months, then annually).
- **Hormonal Associations**: Meningiomas frequently express progesterone receptors; caution is advised with prolonged high-dose progestin therapies.
